# Enterprise Vendor Master Services Agreements

> **Status:** Sample procurement guidance for this application, not an approved
> contract template or legal advice. The organization's Legal and Procurement
> teams must review the actual agreement and approved clause standards before
> execution.

## 1. Purpose and scope

This guidance covers master services agreements (MSAs) and related statements
of work, order forms, amendments, and renewal documents with enterprise
suppliers. The MSA sets the general relationship terms; a statement of work or
order form should define the specific services, deliverables, fees, dates, and
business owners.

An MSA does not by itself authorize spend. Each purchase must also satisfy the
Corporate Procurement Policy, budget controls, and required purchase-order
approvals.

## 2. Review and execution

Send supplier paper and proposed changes to Procurement and Legal before
accepting terms. Include Information Security and Privacy when the service
handles organizational data, accesses systems, or creates material operational
risk. The assigned service owner must confirm that scope and operating
requirements are understood.

Only an authorized signatory may execute an agreement for the organization.
Do not sign, click to accept, begin paid work, enable production access, or
make a verbal commitment before required reviews, approvals, and purchase-order
steps are complete. Keep the final executed version and related approvals in
the approved contract repository.

## 3. Commercial and operational terms

The agreement and its ordering documents should clearly identify:

- the contracting entities, service scope, deliverables, service owner, and
  support responsibilities;
- pricing, currency, usage measures, invoicing, taxes, and any price-change
  mechanism;
- the initial term, renewal mechanics, notice owner, and termination rights;
- service levels, escalation routes, maintenance notices, and continuity
  expectations where relevant; and
- the order of precedence among the MSA, statements of work, order forms, and
  incorporated supplier terms.

Resolve blank fields, inconsistent pricing, unilateral terms, and conflicts
between documents before execution. Do not rely on an informal quote or
marketing page to change signed terms.

## 4. Data, security, and intellectual property

Where applicable, the agreement should address confidentiality, permitted data
use, data ownership, security safeguards, incident notice and cooperation,
subprocessors, audit evidence, data location, retention, return, and deletion.
Privacy and Legal determine whether a data-processing addendum or other
specialized terms are required.

Define ownership and permitted use of deliverables, pre-existing materials,
software, and organization data with Legal. Do not assume that a supplier may
use organization data to train or improve a general-purpose service.

## 5. Risk allocation and termination

Legal reviews warranties, indemnities, insurance, liability limits and
exclusions, dispute terms, governing law, and other risk-allocation provisions.
Requesters and service owners must not negotiate or accept these terms on
behalf of Legal.

Before signing, identify how the organization can suspend or terminate the
service, transition to another provider, export its data, revoke access, and
verify deletion. Capture renewal and notice dates with an accountable owner;
do not allow a renewal deadline to pass without a documented review.

## 6. Amendments and renewals

Changes to scope, price, term, data use, subprocessors, or service
responsibilities must be reviewed before they take effect. Reconfirm budget,
supplier status, security posture, and approval requirements at renewal.
Retain the amendment with the agreement it changes.
