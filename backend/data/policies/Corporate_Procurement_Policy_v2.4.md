# Corporate Procurement Policy v2.4

> **Status:** Sample policy content for this application. It is not an approved
> corporate policy or delegation-of-authority schedule. The organization must
> confirm its approval limits, sourcing rules, and named control owners before
> relying on this document for real purchases.

## 1. Purpose and scope

This policy establishes a controlled process for requesting and acquiring
business goods and services. It applies to employees and other people acting
for the organization, including purchases made through purchase orders,
contracts, subscriptions, trials, renewals, and marketplace listings.

Use the approved procurement process and systems. A purchase must have a
documented business purpose, an accountable requester, an authorized cost
center, and available budget before a commitment is made.

## 2. Core requirements

1. Use an active, approved supplier and an approved catalog item when one is
   available. An approved supplier alone does not make every product, price,
   or use acceptable.
2. Confirm budget and cost-center eligibility with the authoritative finance
   or ERP record. Do not infer a balance from a prior request or a conversation.
3. Obtain required procurement, budget-owner, manager, security, privacy, and
   legal reviews before committing to a purchase.
4. Create and obtain approval for the required purchase order or contract
   before placing an order, enabling a service, or accepting a supplier's
   terms.
5. Keep supporting quotes, approvals, contract records, and exceptions in the
   approved recordkeeping system.

## 3. Request and sourcing

A request should identify the business need, requested goods or service,
quantity or subscription scope, delivery or start date, cost center, budget
owner, and any data, security, integration, or accessibility needs.

Procurement determines the appropriate sourcing process under the
organization's approved sourcing and delegation schedules. When competition
is required, retain the offers considered and the rationale for selection.
If a suitable approved product or supplier cannot be found, refer the request
to Procurement; do not treat an unlisted supplier as approved.

Requesters must disclose actual or potential conflicts of interest. Gifts,
personal benefits, side agreements, and split transactions intended to avoid
review are prohibited. Do not divide a purchase across orders, dates, cost
centers, or suppliers to evade a limit or approval.

## 4. Budget and approval

The cost center and available budget must be verified before an order is
committed. If the ERP lookup is unavailable, the balance is missing, or the
request would exceed available funds, pause and obtain confirmation from the
budget owner or Finance. Do not substitute an estimated balance.

Requests whose total exceeds the configured automated approval threshold must
receive manager approval through the approval workflow before fulfillment. The
application default is **$500 USD**; deployments may configure a different
threshold. A stricter approval limit in the organization's delegation
schedule, budget rules, or another applicable policy takes precedence.

Approval applies only to the reviewed request, amount, supplier, and scope.
Material changes to quantity, price, term, supplier, or use require renewed
review. An approval does not replace required budget, procurement, security,
privacy, or legal approval.

## 5. Contracting and purchase orders

Only authorized signatories may bind the organization. Requesters must not
accept click-through terms, start paid service, sign supplier documents, or
promise a purchase before the required approvals and contract review are
complete.

The purchase order must accurately represent the approved supplier, catalog
items or scope, quantity, unit price, currency, cost center, and total. Resolve
discrepancies before submitting it. Do not represent a draft as an approved or
placed order.

## 6. Security, privacy, and responsible use

Purchases involving organizational data, system access, hosted services, or
software integrations require the reviews described in the Approved Vendor
Security Manual and applicable privacy and technology standards. Do not use a
personal account or unapproved service to avoid review.

## 7. Exceptions, records, and questions

Exceptions require a documented business justification, risk assessment, named
approver, and expiration or review date. Obtain approvals from the control
owners affected by the exception. No exception may authorize unlawful conduct
or bypass an applicable security or legal requirement.

Retain sourcing evidence, approvals, contracts, purchase orders, and exception
records under the organization's retention schedule. Questions about sourcing
or supplier eligibility go to Procurement; budget questions go to Finance;
security and privacy questions go to their respective review teams.
